Budgets

What an assistant needs to answer budget questions about a proposal or an award: the questions, what a budget is as data, the reference tables it is checked against, the checks, and the tools. Part of it is built: the facts table, the budget engine and budget_compute (merged 2026-11-02). The rest is proposed.

Written 2026-11-01; updated 2026-11-02 once decisions 0024 (facts as reviewed values) and 0025 (budgets: one calculator before UVMClick) were accepted and the first tools built. Companion to tools-skills-mcp.html, section 1.4. The budget model was drawn from SPA's Post Award Budget Review workbook, checked against five real UVM proposals the owner provided (kept outside git; findings/2026-11-02-budget-handcheck/). UVM's rates and identifiers come from pages the requirements corpus holds. Where this page and the decisions differ, the decisions win.

1. What horizon holds today

A budget appears in two places, and neither is data a tool can compute on:

  • A checklist task of kind budget, drafted from the call's documents, such as "a budget justification of at most 5 pages".
  • A file in the team's workspace in Teams, which draft_read can read as text.

Before 2026-11-02 an assistant asked "is this budget realistic?" read the budget file as text, found UVM's rates with uvm_requirements_search, and did the arithmetic itself: the arithmetic a model gets wrong. Now it can call budget_compute, which reads the rates from the facts table and does the arithmetic as UVMClick does.

UVM already has a budgeting system. SPA's fact sheet says "UVMClick proposal budgeting system will calculate the fringe automatically, using the most current rates in effect at the time of proposal creation." UVMClick is UVM's system of record for a proposal's budget. So horizon's budget is a draft and a check before the budget goes into UVMClick, or a reading of UVMClick's budget if horizon can ever reach it. It is not a second budgeting system: decision 0025, item 1, ruled the first.

2. The questions

Four kinds of question, each answered by a different mix of data, rules and judgment.

QuestionWho asksWhat answers it
"Build me a three-year budget: me at one summer month, a postdoc, a GRA, travel to two conferences."PI, with SPA UVM's chart of accounts, fringe rates and F&A rate, and the arithmetic. Deterministic once the inputs are entered.
"Does this budget follow UVM's rules?"SPA, PI The same reference tables, as checks: a fringe rate missing for a year, an F&A rate that matches none of UVM's, an unknown account, effort below what was promised.
"Does it fit the call?"SPA, PI The sponsor's rules: a cap on the total or on indirect costs, a salary cap, costs the call does not allow. These live in the call's checklist and the funder's guides, as text today.
"Is it realistic given the narrative?"PI, RD, SPA Judgment: effort against who owns which aim, cost categories against the activities the narrative describes, size against funded awards in the program. A model with a skill, never a tool.
"Which of my awards will end with money unspent?"PI, SPA post-award The award's budget with its actual and projected spending, and its end date.

3. What a budget is

Project years, each with lines in UVM's own accounts, and the people whose salaries the budget pays. Everything else is computed.

The parts, as built (budget-1)

PartWhat it holdsNotes
BudgetThe sponsor's F&A rate as entered (or cap, or 0), the base (MTDC or TDC), the location and purpose (the rate agreement's words), an escalation factor per period, a label. The F&A rate is the budget's own; UVM's agreement rate is looked up and shown beside it.
PeriodOne project year: id, label, start and end date. At most ten.
LineAn id, a period, a Flevel, a description and an amount in whole dollars. The spending columns (encumbrances, expenditures, projected) wait for awards.
SubawardAn id and an amount per period. The engine puts the first $25,000 of each in an MTDC base, across periods in order.
PersonA person_key ("lead", "postdoc-1"), never a name; an employee type (the agreement's six classes); and per period either person-months in thousandths (with appointment months and base salary today) or a flat wage. Salary and fringe are computed. A flat wage is for hourly students and technicians.

UVM's chart of accounts

Lines are keyed by Flevel, UVM's sponsored-budget chart of accounts: 34 codes, of which SPA's public work instructions for budget transfer journals (Appendix A) list 33 with their F&A treatment; the 34th is F6600, the F&A line. Each has one of three F&A types:

In the base (25)

Direct costs that bear F&A on an MTDC award: salary (F5000), fringe (F5990), supplies, travel (F6050, F6052), publication costs, consultants and more.

Excluded from the base (8)

Participant costs (F6101), patient care, facility and equipment rental, acquisitions, equipment over $5,000 (F6500), tuition (F6532), alterations and renovations.

The last code, F6600, is the F&A line itself. A subaward is not split by hand between two Flevels: the engine computes its part in the base. SPA's per-category guidance is spa/direct-cost-budget-categories, which the requirements corpus holds.

What is stored and what is computed

Salary, fringe and F&A are never stored. They are computed from the people and lines each time, so they cannot drift from their inputs. Revised budget and available balance are computed too. The budget stores only what a person entered.

4. The arithmetic

As UVMClick computes a proposal budget, pinned by the engine's tests and checked against five real proposals: AWD#2492 reproduces UVMClick's totals exactly, the others within a few dollars a line (decision 0025, A7). Whole dollars, integer arithmetic, rounded half away from zero.

monthly rate       = base salary today × (1 + escalation of period 1) ÷ appointment months
salary, period 1   = round(monthly rate × person-months)
salary, period n   = round(salary of period n−1 × (1 + escalation of period n))   same months
fringe             = round(salary × the fringe rate of the person's type), per person and period
base (MTDC)        = salaries + fringe + lines in the base + each subaward's first $25,000
F&A               = round(base × the budget's own F&A rate), per period
F&A forgone       = round(base × UVM's agreement rate) − F&A
calculated rate    = F&A ÷ base
  • One rate for every year. SPA: "Do not escalate F&A costs or Fringe costs. The approved and published rates at the time of proposal must be used for F&A and fringe." The engine takes the rates in effect on the first period's start; which date fixes them is open with SPA (question 9). Charging each fiscal year's actual rate is how an award is spent, not how a proposal is budgeted.
  • The budget's rate, beside UVM's. A sponsor's cap or "no indirect costs" is the budget's rate; UVM's agreement rate for the location and purpose is shown beside it with the F&A forgone. Neither number is corrected.
  • A TDC budget puts every direct cost in the base, subawards whole.
  • Escalation is a factor per period, from the first: SPA recommends up to 4%, and the engine warns above it.
  • Still open (A6, A7): whether UVMClick compounds on the rounded previous year or the unrounded rate, whether it rounds fringe per person or on the sum, a person whose months change between years, a nine-month faculty member's monthly rate, and one escalation for the whole budget where workbooks raise named people and leave a hire not yet made at today's rate.

5. The facts a budget needs

Three kinds of fact, each with its own home: UVM's, the person's, and the budget's own.

UVM's facts are published, and horizon already crawls them

The requirements corpus holds the pages that state UVM's rates and identifiers. They are public pages and PDFs on www.uvm.edu, so their values are Low.

Page in the corpusWhat it states
spa/rate-agreement-0UVM's federal rate agreement, the PDF the fact sheet links. F&A rates by location and purpose with effective dates, such as on-campus research 53.0% for FY25 and 53.5% for FY26. Fringe rates by employee type with effective dates: Regular Employees 52.5% for FY25 and 55.4% for FY26, and UVM Medical Group, Graduate Students, Temporary Employees, Student Employees and Post Docs.
spa/f-a-ratesThe F&A table by fiscal year from FY15 to FY27, FY27 marked provisional. On-campus research is 53.5% for FY27.
spa/spa-fact-sheetUVM's legal entity, addresses, signing officials, federal tax ids, UEI and the non-profit letter. Its fringe rule: "All proposals must use the federally approved fringe benefit rates in effect at the time the proposal is submitted", and the rates are on page 3 of the rate agreement.
finance/benefit-ratesWhich employee types get which benefit rate, and that SPA receives future-year projected rates for multi-year proposals.

The SPA fact sheet is the targeted document a budget needs, but as text. Horizon would turn it into data the way it turns a call's documents into checklist items. Each value (a rate, the EIN, the UEI, a signing official) is stored with the sentence it was read from, its source key and its effective dates. A value past its last effective date is not offered, and a provisional rate is served with its type.

These facts have two readers: budgets, and every form that asks for UVM's EIN or UEI. The uvm-requirements skill already warns against copying an EIN from a stale aggregator. A fact lookup fixes that at the source, so it earns its own tool, facts_read (section 7).

Decision 0024, accepted and partly built. One table of facts, one row per value: 18 rows today (backend/dbt/seeds/facts.csv, served as funding.facts), all not_reviewed until SPA's first round. Each row holds a scope (UVM, a funder guide or a call), a name from a fixed vocabulary (fa_rate, fringe_rate, ein, uei, salary_cap, indirect_cap ...), the value and its conditions, its effective dates, the source's own sentence, and the source's sha256. A row is proposed by a person or a model and confirmed by SPA. Every build checks each row against its source. A quote no longer in the source is never served. A changed source or an unconfirmed row is served marked.

No value enters without the funder's or UVM's own sentence from a document horizon holds. So Sloan's indirect cap stays out until horizon holds Sloan's own guideline: a secondary source says 15% above $50,000, and sloan.org refuses our fetch.

For a GRA, spa/direct-cost-budget-categories holds the GRA salary table, the comprehensive fees ("Use the amounts below for all budget periods, until otherwise changed") and the tuition rule; they are candidate facts, not yet rows.

A person's facts are entered per budget, never as a rate

A budget needs two facts about each UVM person it pays:

  • Appointment months, 9 to 12. It decides whether summer salary exists and how a monthly rate is computed.
  • Employee type: regular, UVM Medical Group, post doc, graduate student and so on. It decides the fringe type, and the fringe rate follows from the type and the fiscal year.

Decision 0025 enters both per budget and per person (the owner's ruling). A budget pays several people, and a profile describes only the person signed in. budget_compute stores nothing, so the values arrive in the call, and the skill asks for them ("a 9- or 12-month appointment?"). A profile field next to academic_rank and appointment_percent can come later as a default, once a stored budget exists and a tool reads it, and UVM's HR job data if a source and a tier ruling appear. A fringe rate is never stored on a person, because it changes every July 1. The budget holds the employee type, and the rate is looked up for each fiscal year.

Salary is entered in the budget, not the profile

Salary is sensitive, changes every year, and a person may not want their assistant to hold it in general. It is entered per budget and per person, and a first draft can leave it as a placeholder for the person to fill. People the budget pays who are not hired yet, such as "a postdoc" or "a GRA", are roles: each role gives a fringe type, and its salary is a reference fact or an amount the person enters.

The example, step by step

"Build me a three-year budget: me at one summer month, a postdoc, a GRA, travel to two conferences."

  1. Me. The skill asks for my appointment months and employee type: a 9-month appointment, a regular employee. Salary is entered or left as a placeholder. One summer month a year goes on F5000, and fringe on F5990 at Regular Employees' rate for each fiscal year.
  2. The postdoc. A role with the Post Docs fringe type, 12 months, at the salary the person enters or the postdoc floor.
  3. The GRA. The Graduate Students fringe type and a stipend, plus tuition on F6532, which is outside the F&A base.
  4. Travel. Two lines on F6050 each year.
  5. Rates. budget_compute reads F&A and fringe from the facts table, one rate for every year, as of the first period's start. A missing rate is reported, never guessed. Each rate in the reply names its page and the sentence it came from.
  6. The draft. The totals, the checks, and the note that SPA enters the final budget in UVMClick.

UVM's chart of accounts

The chart's codes and F&A types are public (SPA's budget transfer journal instructions, Appendix A), so the chart is Low (decision 0025, item 9).

6. The checks

A check either finds a fact a person must confirm or does arithmetic a model can get wrong. None changes a number. Three groups, by what each needs.

Built: against UVM's facts and the budget itself

KindWhen it fires
no_periodsThe budget has no project years.
no_fringe_rateNo fact gives the rate for a person's employee type. Fringe is zero with the reason, never a guessed rate.
no_fa_rateNo fact gives UVM's rate for the budget's location and purpose; the budget's own rate is used and nothing is compared.
no_subaward_limitNo fact gives the subaward limit; the F&A on up to $25,000 per subaward may be missing.
unknown_flevelA line's account is not in the chart. It counts in the total but in no base.
under_committedA person is budgeted fewer person-months than committed.
fa_rate_off_tableThe budget's F&A rate differs from UVM's for its location and purpose. Fine if it is the sponsor's limit; both are shown.
rate_not_reviewedA fact used is not confirmed by SPA.
escalation_over_recommendedA period's escalation is above SPA's recommended 4%.

Against the call proposed

These need the sponsor's budget rules as facts with a call or guide scope (decision 0025, item 8; 0024's addendum: typed from the call's own checklist items, any category). None is held yet, and budget_compute says so when given a call_id.

  • The total, or a year, over the call's ceiling.
  • Indirect costs over a sponsor's cap.
  • Salary over a sponsor's salary cap, such as NIH's.
  • Senior personnel salary over NSF's limit on months per year, from the PAPPG.
  • A line in a category the call does not allow.

Each would quote the sentence it rests on, as checklist items do, and name the guide edition in effect. A rule the call states only in prose stays a judgment until someone writes it as data.

Judgment, left to the model

  • Does each person's effort fit what the narrative gives them?
  • Does each activity in the narrative have a cost, and each cost an activity?
  • How does the total compare with funded awards in the same program?

7. The tools

Following tools-skills-mcp.html's rule: a read returns the record with its checks, and a write groups actions that have the same consequence.

It returns the inputs as one table, the totals per period (the budget's rate, UVM's, the F&A forgone), the checks, each what-if's differences, and each fact used with its quote. 8. Where the logic lives

The arithmetic lives in one tool. The workbook is what people look at and edit. The skill holds the method.

Three places logic can live

MechanismWhere it runsWho gets itGood for
A tool on horizon's MCP server Horizon's server. Every client that connects: Claude Code, Claude Desktop, claude.ai, Cursor, an agent on an open-weights model. Answers that must be the same for everyone and correct: the arithmetic, UVM's rates by fiscal year, the checks, writing to Teams as the person.
A skill with scripts The client's own sandbox. Anthropic's own xlsx skill works this way. Python with openpyxl writes the workbook, and a LibreOffice step recalculates its formulas. Only clients that load skills and can run code: Claude Code, and claude.ai with code execution. Producing a file and formatting it. The numbers it computes are whatever the script's copy of the rules says.
A file in a workspace: an Excel workbook in Teams Microsoft's Excel, reached through Microsoft Graph, as draft_read reaches a team's drafts today. People in Teams and Excel, and any agent with a tool that reads and writes it. Where people already work. They see the formulas, change a cell and watch the totals move.

JSON is not a fourth mechanism. It is the shape of a budget (years, lines by Flevel, people, the changes of a what-if) that the three share, and defining it once is what lets them agree.

The line between a tool and a skill

  • A tool does what must be right and shared: the arithmetic, UVM's rates, the checks, and reading or writing the team's workbook as the person. A skill's script would be a second copy of the arithmetic, on someone else's machine, with the rates it shipped with, and invisible to an outside user whose client cannot run code.
  • A skill holds the method: which questions to ask before building ("a 9- or 12-month appointment?"), which what-ifs are worth trying near a cap, how to present a check, and when to say SPA enters the final budget in UVMClick. It can use the xlsx skill to hand a person a formatted file, whose numbers come from the tool.
  • The workbook is the team's working view. It does not hold the rules.

The design, in five parts

  1. The budget's shape is the contract, as JSON: budget-1, years, lines by Flevel, subawards and people. built
  2. budget_compute is the one authority for the arithmetic. It is stateless and Low. A budget plus what-if changes go in, and totals and checks come out, with each what-if's difference beside the base. It stores nothing, and the tool layer never logs a call's arguments, so a salary passed in a call is not kept. built
  3. The platform makes an Excel workbook in the team's Teams folder, as it makes Markdown drafts today. Horizon owns the template, so it can read the workbook back, which solves the problem that every team's spreadsheet differs. The workbook can carry formulas for people to tweak by hand. On reading it back, horizon recomputes with the engine and reports each cell where the workbook disagrees, and never corrects it silently. dropped for now (decision 0025, C5): a third budget beside UVMClick and the sponsor's forms, a stored workbook with salaries needs a tier ruling, and a Graph write into an open workbook needs a hand test.
  4. budget_edit writes the inputs through Graph, as draft_edit writes a section, and never the computed cells. with the workbook
  5. The budget-build skill (built) and a later budget-review hold the method and call the tools.

Three copies of the arithmetic. The engine, the workbook's formulas and any skill script can drift apart. One authority, and a check of the others against it on read-back, is how horizon's AGENTS.md treats a copy: a row in backend/tests/test_copies.py.

A person editing while the agent writes. The Markdown editor once saved over an upload while someone typed, in a hand test on 2026-10-26. Excel Online's co-authoring may handle a Graph write into an open workbook better. That is not verified, and budget_edit needs a hand test before it writes into a workbook people have open.

UVMClick stays the record. The Teams workbook is the team's working copy before SPA enters the budget in UVMClick, and an export from UVMClick could seed it.

9. The skill

A problem-first budget-review skill, from the worked example, now with the checks above:

  1. Call budget_read. Report every check, and say which are rule violations and which ask a person to confirm.
  2. Call checklist_read and documents_search for the call's budget rules. Compare the totals with each one, quoting it.
  3. Call draft_read on the narrative. Compare each person's effort with the aims they own, and each activity with a cost line.
  4. Compare the total with funded awards in the program, once awards_read can list them.
  5. Label each finding as a UVM rule, a sponsor rule, a comparison or an opinion.

What the skill teaches that no tool says: a rate that differs from UVM's table may be the sponsor's limit and correct; a provisional rate is not wrong; a missing fringe rate is not zero fringe.

The order to build in

  1. The facts table and the engine. built
  2. budget_compute and the budget-build skill. built
  3. facts_read, the facts alarms, and SPA's first review round.
  4. budget_compare and its keyed benchmark, from the five proposals' findings.
  5. A stored working copy and a workbook, once item 6's tier ruling and the Graph hand test are done; then awards.

11. Ruled, and still open

Ruled by the owner on 2026-11-01 (decisions 0024 and 0025):

  • Horizon's budget is a calculator and working copy before SPA enters the budget in UVMClick, the record.
  • The tool is facts_read; whoever SPA names confirms facts, recorded as the office and the date.
  • Appointment months and employee type are entered per budget and per person.
  • Colleagues' salaries may go to the caller's model provider for the call only, nothing stored, the skill advising a provider UVM has a contract with.
  • No scaffolded workbook and no generated numbers in the justification, for now.

Still open:

  • For SPA (decision 0025): the subaward and equipment thresholds against the federal $50,000 and $10,000 (question 8); which date fixes the rates (9); a nine-month faculty member's monthly rate (11); and the two rounding questions of A7.
  • For the owner: the addendum's A-1 to A-3 (budget_compare as an account tool, a model reading the justification, the category mapping's tier), and the tier of a stored budget with salaries.
